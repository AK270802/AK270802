Layer 03 / Activations

Systems with real users, real constraints, real ownership.

Four bodies of work. Each one framed the way it was actually built: problem, approach, architecture, and what my role in it was.

ACT·01

Enterprise AI Growth Platform

In production

Six-module SaaS suite for marketing and growth AI at Baseel Partners, delivered across four markets.

Problem

Enterprise clients across UK, India, UAE and KSA needed marketing intelligence — content, intent, conversion, campaigns, insight — but each capability built separately would mean six disconnected products and six data models.

Approach

One platform, six modules (GrowthOS, AuthorityAI, IntentPulse, ConversionAI, CampaignPilot, InsightFabric) on shared infrastructure: a common feature store, a common model lifecycle, and agentic LLM pipelines with per-module contracts instead of one monolithic agent.

Architecture & stack

Django · Celery · Airflow orchestration · Feast feature store · MLflow lifecycle · XGBoost + Prophet forecasting · multi-agent LLM pipelines · AWS · Kubernetes

Role & scope

AI Lead Engineer — platform architecture end to end, plus leadership of the 8–10 engineer delivery team. I own the architectural decisions and answer for them in front of clients.

ACT·02

LexAI — Legal AI SaaS

Independently architected

Document intelligence and legal drafting for Irish and UK law firms in the 2–15 solicitor range — the practices too small for enterprise legal tech and too busy for manual review.

Problem

Small practices handle the same contract review, discovery and drafting loads as large firms, without the staff. Existing legal AI tools price and integrate for the top of the market.

Approach

A retrieval-first system: firm documents are parsed, embedded and grounded before any generation happens, with LangGraph agents handling multi-step workflows (intake → research → draft → cite-check) rather than a single chat prompt. Tiered pricing matches practice size.

Architecture & stack

Django 5 + DRF · Next.js 14 · Qdrant vector store · LangGraph orchestration · Docling parsing · BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings · MinIO storage · Celery/Redis async pipeline

Role & scope

Sole architect — end to end, from the async ingestion pipeline to the pricing model. Every component above was a deliberate selection against alternatives, not a default.

ACT·03

ShadowHunter — AI Security & Governance

Design cycle complete

An AI security and governance platform positioned directly against category incumbents — taken from market analysis to buildable specification.

Problem

Organisations adopting AI need visibility into what models, agents and dependencies are actually running — and the incumbent platforms are priced and shaped for a segment most buyers aren't in.

Approach

Full product design cycle: product requirements (PRP), competitive analysis against the incumbents, and a 14-screen interactive prototype. Then depth on one wedge: Kestrel, an SBOM & vulnerability management module scoped to a 14-week delivery plan.

Architecture & stack

Kestrel module: FastAPI microservice · Syft SBOM generation · Grype vulnerability scanning · 14-week phased delivery plan

Role & scope

Product and technical lead for the design cycle — the market positioning, the screens, and the engineering plan a team could execute without me in the room.

ACT·04

Public Sector Tender Delivery

Submitted frameworks

Technical proposals for government and semi-state tenders — digital transformation and AI training frameworks.

Problem

Public sector AI procurement demands technical requirement documents, delivery plans and compliance mappings precise enough to be contractually binding — a different discipline from product engineering.

Approach

Architected the proposed solutions and wrote the technical volumes myself: requirement traceability, phased delivery plans, resourcing models and risk registers that survive procurement scrutiny.

Role & scope

Technical author and solution architect for submitted bids on government and semi-state frameworks.

Why it matters

Lead engineers who can carry a system from architecture diagram to a signed public framework are rare — it's the same rigour, pointed at paperwork with legal weight.
