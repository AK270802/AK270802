Layer 01 / How the system was trained

Formed on real constraints, not clean datasets.

The path in

I started in AI before it was the obvious choice: a BE in Artificial Intelligence & Data Science, taken when most curricula still treated ML as an elective. During that degree I co-built Clearway Companion, an AI assistive wearable for visually impaired users, published with Elsevier — my first lesson that a model is worthless until it works on a device, on a street, for a person who depends on it.

An MSc in Data Science & Analytics at Munster Technological University brought me to Cork, and a McKinsey Forward Fellowship added the layer most engineers skip: how businesses actually make decisions, allocate budgets and measure outcomes. That combination — deep technical work plus commercial literacy — is what pushed me toward platform leadership rather than pure research.

What I lead now

At Baseel Partners — a UK-headquartered software and consulting firm working across cybersecurity, AI/ML, cloud and compliance in the UK, India, UAE and KSA, with Cork as its EU delivery and R&D base — I'm the AI Lead Engineer responsible for a six-module enterprise AI SaaS platform.

That means owning the architecture end to end: agentic multi-LLM pipelines, XGBoost and Prophet forecasting, a Feast feature store, MLflow model lifecycle, Airflow orchestration, all on AWS and Kubernetes — and leading the 8–10 engineers who build and operate it. Alongside that, I've architected a legal AI SaaS independently and consulted on AI compliance tooling for a Saudi-based firm.

Operating philosophy

Deployment is the product. A demo that impresses in a meeting and an agent that runs unattended in a customer's tenancy are different disciplines. I design for the second: observability, rollback paths, cost ceilings and failure modes before features.

Governance is an architecture concern, not a slide. Working across regulated markets — and on compliance products directly — taught me to treat auditability, data residency and model accountability as first-class system requirements, designed in at the pipeline level.

Agentic systems need boundaries. Multi-agent LLM orchestration only earns its complexity when each agent has a narrow contract, a measurable output and a supervisor that can say no. Everything else is expensive nondeterminism.
